## FaultBench-OTel: a real system, real telemetry (pre-registered 2026-10-16)

Every other number on this page comes from worlds our own simulator generates. FaultBench-OTel ([`evals/faultbench_otel.py`](../evals/faultbench_otel.py)) is the first test on a running system: the official OpenTelemetry Demo, its own flagd fault flags as the causes, and its measured span-metric error rates (Prometheus) as the only telemetry the product sees. Pre-registered before the first injection in [PREREGISTRATION.md](../evals/PREREGISTRATION.md) (addendum 2026-10-16 23:00 IST, with two dated amendments; first committed to git on 2026-10-17 at 22:19 IST, after most windows had run, so its timing rests on our own record). Label it plainly: **real services and real telemetry; our own choice of fault flags, our own decoy changes and page rule; one laptop.** <!-- noclaim: dates and times -->

**Set-up.** Demo release `3.1.0` (pinned, never `latest`), trimmed to the core services, flagd, the load generator (25 HTTP users) and Prometheus: about 1.1 GB of memory, about 2 GB of image downloads ([`deploy/otel-demo/`](../deploy/otel-demo/), `scripts/otel_demo.sh`). **What it found.**
- **A real bug our simulated benches could not show.** On the first fault window the product said "no incident" while the ad service was failing at 7–15 %. `metric_window` computed its ratio only for a non-zero baseline and required a ratio to call a measurement anomalous; healthy services in the real demo report exactly zero errors (the simulator never does), so every rise from zero read as normal and the negative proof accepted NO_INCIDENT. The pre-registered product ("as registered" above) got <!-- claim:otel.faultline_prefix.right_cause.k -->0<!-- /claim --> causes right and called <!-- claim:otel.faultline_prefix.no_incident_fault -->15<!-- /claim --> real faults "no incident". The fix (a rise of at least `gate.err_abs_min` from zero is outside every band) makes "no incident" harder to accept and changes no gate condition, weight or threshold; because it was found on these windows, both versions are run on every window and both are reported (amendment 2026-10-16 23:45 IST). <!-- noclaim: observed rates quoted from the window, amendment date -->
- **Where it abstains, and why.** A fault that hangs instead of failing: before a stack restart, `cartFailure` made checkout stall (order throughput collapsed) and showed up only as timeouts at the edge proxy, with cart's own error rate near zero; an error-rate-only product cannot place that origin, so Faultline abstained and named the innocent no-op deploy on the proxy as a "not confident" suspect, while T and T+ blamed it outright. After the restart the same flag failed fast, cart's errors were visible, and the cause was confirmed. Per-minute timing: three times (two `productCatalogFailure` windows, one `paymentFailure` window) a low-volume client (checkout) paged a minute before the high-volume origin, so at the page the origin's own measurement was still in band and the gate escalated instead of claiming. `paymentUnreachable` always ended "not confident" with the true cause named: an earlier change upstream on the path (a decoy) keeps the claim from being confident even after that decoy is eliminated, a conservative rule (G3 earlier upstream). <!-- noclaim: description -->
- **The heuristics.** T+ is right where the cause's own service carries the biggest rise (ad, most payment windows) and wrong where a decoy sits on a service that inherits the errors: it blamed the harmless flag flip on checkout in every `productCatalogFailure` window and in one `paymentFailure` window, and the no-op deploy on the edge proxy in both hanging `cartFailure` windows. One control page landed on a real stray checkout error, and T+ blamed that window's harmless flag flip; Faultline accepted the same change only as a "not confident" suspect. Naive recency does well on fault windows because the flip lands a minute or two before the page, so the cause is usually the newest change; it blames a change on every control. <!-- noclaim: description -->
